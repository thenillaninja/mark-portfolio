This fictional example shows how one Snapshot recommendation could become connected operating knowledge instead of disappearing after the initial assessment.

EXAMPLE BUSINESS Cedar Ridge Landscaping

Small residential landscaping company with a recurring scheduling, job-close, and customer follow-up workflow.

Snapshot → Decision → Action → Outcome → Lesson → System
01 / SNAPSHOT

Business learning was identified as a weak point.

Useful lessons were often discussed after jobs, but rarely captured in a way the business could use later.

02 / DECISION

Capture one lesson after meaningful work.

The business chose to add a short review after completed jobs where something important went especially well or poorly.

03 / ACTION

A weekly project-close review was added.

Completed jobs were reviewed for delays, scope changes, customer issues, and anything the team would handle differently next time.

04 / OUTCOME

Recurring handoff problems became easier to spot.

Several scheduling issues were traced back to scope changes being discussed in the field but not reflected in the office schedule.

05 / LESSON

Confirm scope changes before scheduling follow-up work.

Field changes should be confirmed and recorded before another crew, delivery, or return visit is scheduled.

06 / SYSTEM

The lesson became part of the project-close process.

Scope-change confirmation was added to the standard closeout checklist so future jobs could begin with the lesson already built into the operating process.

THE DOJO MIND DIFFERENCE

The recommendation was temporary. The lesson became reusable business context.

In the full Business Dojo, Dojo Mind can preserve the relationships between what was observed, what was tried, what happened, and what the business learned over time.
